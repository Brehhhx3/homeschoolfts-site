The Digital Planning Setup That Changed How I Run Our Household

September 19, 2026 · Smart Living

I used to keep way too much in my head.

Appointments. School assignments. Homeschool plans. Website ideas. Things I needed to buy. Emails I needed to answer. Random reminders I was absolutely convinced I would remember later.

I did not remember them later.

For a long time, I thought I needed a better planner. What I actually needed was a better system.

Not a perfect system. Not one of those ridiculously organized setups with 14 dashboards and a color-coded button for every part of your life.

I needed somewhere for everything to go.

That changed the way I run our household.

The Biggest Change: I Stopped Using One Tool for Everything

I used to think I needed one perfect app that could handle every part of my life.

That sounded nice in theory. In reality, I kept trying new apps, setting everything up, getting overwhelmed, and abandoning them.

Now I let different tools do different jobs. That made everything much easier.

Calendar for time. Task manager for things I need to do. Project system for bigger work. Notes or documents for information I need to keep.

Once I stopped forcing everything into one place, the whole setup started making more sense.

Google Calendar Holds the Stuff That Happens at a Specific Time

If something happens on a certain day or at a certain time, it goes on the calendar.

That includes:

  • Appointments
  • Homeschool activities
  • School deadlines
  • Events and meetings
  • Testing dates
  • Things I absolutely cannot forget
  • Anything that affects the rest of the day's schedule

I also like separating things into different calendars so I can quickly tell what I am looking at. Homeschool can have its own color. School can have another. Family stuff can have another.

I do not need to memorize everything anymore. I just need to check the calendar.

My Task List Holds the Things That Do Not Need a Calendar

Not everything needs to be scheduled for 2:15 on Tuesday.

Some things just need to get done.

Things like:

  • Order printer ink
  • Return library books
  • Update a website page
  • Print next week's homeschool sheets
  • Email someone back
  • Research a new curriculum
  • Fix something on HomeSchoolFTS
  • Buy whatever random thing one of the kids needs tomorrow

This is one of the biggest ways I have reduced the mental load. When I remember something, I put it somewhere.

I do not stand in the kitchen repeating "printer ink, printer ink, printer ink" to myself because I know the second somebody asks me a question, printer ink is gone forever.

Monday.com Is Where Bigger Projects Live

This is where I separate everyday tasks from actual projects.

A task might be update a product description.

A project is create and launch a new digital homeschool resource.

Those are not the same thing.

For bigger projects, I need to see:

  • What still needs to be done
  • What is already finished
  • What is waiting on something else
  • What is blocked
  • What comes next

For HomeSchoolFTS, I can keep track of blog posts, digital products, website updates, launches, SEO tasks, Stripe setup, GitHub changes, content ideas, and technical problems.

The key is that I do not put every tiny household task in there. If I did, I would spend more time organizing my life than actually living it.

I Keep Information Separate From Tasks

This was another big shift.

A note is not a task. A link is not a task. A curriculum idea is not automatically a project.

Sometimes information is just information.

I keep things like curriculum links, website ideas, research notes, product ideas, homeschool resources, writing ideas, and reference information somewhere I can find them again.

That keeps my task list from turning into a storage closet.

I Use the "Capture First, Organize Later" Rule

If I have to stop and decide exactly where something belongs every time I remember it, I am probably not going to save it.

So I capture it first. Organize it later.

That might mean throwing something into a quick note, my task inbox, a saved email, or a temporary list.

The important part is getting it out of my head before it disappears.

My System Is Built Around Real Life, Not the Perfect Week

I used to make plans based on the version of the week where nobody got sick, nothing broke, nobody needed me unexpectedly, and I somehow had eight uninterrupted productive hours every day.

That week has never happened.

Now I look at the fixed things first: appointments, school deadlines, kids' activities, homeschool commitments, and anything time-sensitive. Then I decide what else can reasonably fit.

That means I am planning around reality instead of planning a fantasy schedule and feeling behind by Tuesday.

I Separate "Important" From "Would Be Nice"

Not everything deserves the same level of attention.

Some things have deadlines. Some things are blocking other work. Some things directly affect the kids. Some things affect the business. And some things are just ideas I think would be cool someday.

Those should not all be sitting next to each other screaming for attention.

Must happen. Should happen. Would be nice.

That one little distinction makes a giant list feel much less overwhelming.

I Review the System Instead of Rebuilding It

I used to rebuild my planning system every time I felt behind.

New app. New board. New notebook. New color scheme. Fresh start.

Meanwhile, the original problem was still sitting there waiting for me.

Now I try to fix the part that is not working instead of throwing the whole system away.

If I keep forgetting appointments, that is a calendar problem. If tasks keep disappearing, that is a capture problem. If projects feel confusing, that is a project-management problem.

I do not need to burn everything down because Thursday got messy.

What My Setup Actually Looks Like

Calendar
For things tied to a date or time.

Task List
For things I need to do.

Monday.com
For bigger projects and multi-step work.

Notes/Documents
For information, ideas, resources, and things I need to reference later.

That is pretty much it.

The magic is not the apps. It is knowing what belongs where.

The Goal Is Less Mental Clutter

This setup did not magically make our house perfectly organized. That is not even the goal.

The goal is that I do not have to carry everything around in my head.

I can look at the calendar and know what is happening. I can look at my tasks and know what needs attention. I can open a project and see where I left off. I can save an idea without turning it into something I have to deal with immediately.

That frees up a surprising amount of mental space.

And when you are balancing homeschooling, parenting, school, a business, and regular life, mental space is kind of everything.

Start Smaller Than You Think

If your current system is mostly sticky notes, screenshots, half-finished notebooks, and "I will remember that," do not try to build some giant digital command center tonight.

Start with one problem.

If you keep forgetting appointments, fix the calendar. If your to-do list is living in your head, start a task inbox. If you have ten unfinished projects and cannot remember where you left off, use a project board.

Give each tool one clear job.

Then let the system grow only when you actually need it.

That is what finally worked for me.

Not perfect organization.

Just fewer things floating around in my head.
